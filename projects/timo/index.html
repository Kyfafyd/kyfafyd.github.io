01Abstract

Most human motion generators inject text through cross-attention, where motion reads fixed text features but does not update them in return. Multimodal diffusion transformers instead let text and motion tokens refine each other, making their transfer to motion a natural next step.

Articulated motion is temporally coherent but weakly correlated across joints. A direct MMDiT transfer with flow matching produces poorly coordinated, jerky motion: neighboring joints need not share a pose, but their changes over time must remain coordinated.

We therefore propose Timo, a kinematics-aware multimodal diffusion transformer for human motion. Fully shared attention updates text and motion bidirectionally; geometric and rotational-kinematics losses supervise the articulated body and its evolution; and a two-stage curriculum moves from broad motion learning to detailed caption alignment. Timo surpasses Kimodo on five of six axes, with a 40.8% relative improvement in the average score.

03Six-axis evaluation

Each axis is a custom 0–100 score, higher better, so the columns share one scale. Text-Motion Match, Naturalness and Diversity are scored in a contrastive text–motion embedding. Smoothness uses third-order differences of the body-frame angular increment. Plausibility is limb-capsule interpenetration, a geometric proxy rather than a test of balance or dynamics. Speed combines time to the first output and per-segment time at batch size one. The last column is the mean of all six. Timo surpasses Kimodo on five of six axes and raises the average from 61.5 to 86.6, a 40.8% relative improvement. Kimodo remains highest on Plausibility (93.4 vs. 85.0).

05Motion of any length

A longer request reuses the same generator.

Training sees 8-second windows. A longer request is tiled with overlapping windows, and those overlaps are blended inside every sampling step, without growing the attention context.
